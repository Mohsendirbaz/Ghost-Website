- *What Remains**
Hours later, the shoes might be gone. Or they might still be there, passed over by people who can't bear to touch that much sorrow, even to help.
But the image persists:
A quivering teen on Douglas Street in Chicago's brutal winter, holding infant shoes labeled "slightly used," trying to make enough for a meal, for warmth, for whatever comes next.
This is the story that doesn't make the news. The kind Hemingway would have written in six words, or Carver would have stretched across pages of brutal, beautiful restraint. The kind that Chicago knows in its bones but tourists never see.
The shoes - slightly used - carry the weight of a story we can only piece together in fragments. And maybe that's the point. Maybe the power is in what's left unsaid, in the space between "slightly used" and the unbearable math that brought them to a frozen street corner.
The teen shivers. The wind cuts. The shoes wait.
