## PART I: THE INVESTABLE FRONTIER — Institutional Capacity and Resource Allocation Failures

### Chapter 1: Currency, Competitiveness, and the Erosion of National Capacity

**1.1 The Declining Dollar and Fiscal Constraints**
- The dollar's share of global foreign exchange reserves declined from approximately 71% around 2000 to 59% by Q4 2020, marking its lowest level in 25 years
- The trajectory continued through late 2024, reaching 57.8%
- Projections suggest the share could decline further to 40-45% by 2050

**1.2 Middle-Class Earning Power Compression**
- The share of aggregate U.S. household income held by the middle class fell from 62% in 1970 to 42% in 2020
- Meanwhile, the share of aggregate income accounted for by upper-income households increased steadily, from 29% in 1970 to 50% in 2020

**1.3 Downward Mobility**
- The share of adults who live in middle-class households fell from 61% in 1971 to 50% in 2021
- By 2023, the share had fallen further to 51%
- The shrinking of the middle class has been accompanied by an increase in the share of adults in the upper-income tier – from 14% in 1971 to 21% in 2021

**1.4 Measuring National Decline: Composite Indicators**
- Household incomes have risen considerably since 1970, but those of middle-class households have not climbed nearly as much as those of upper-income households. The median income of middle-class households in 2020 was 50% greater than in 1970 ($90,131 vs. $59,934)
- The rise in income from 1970 to 2020 was steepest for upper-income households. Their median income increased 69% during that timespan, from $130,008 to $219,572

**2.1 The 411,549 Teaching Position Crisis**

**2.2 Education, Sugar Intake, and Health Outcome Cascades**

**2.3 The Hidden Tax of Under-Education**
- A 2003 study found that every additional male who graduates high school creates $2,100 in social savings every year by lowering incarceration rates
- Graduation rates from religious schools range from 97 to 99 percent, as compared with 73 percent for public schools

**2.4 International Comparisons: U.S. vs. China**
- Chinese per capita sugar consumption reaches 5.76 kilograms annually compared to the U.S. figure of 33.7 kilograms, representing a nearly six-fold differential

### Chapter 3: The Digital Divide and Rural Economic Exclusion

**3.1 Broadband Access Disparities**
- More than twenty-two percent of rural Americans lack access to basic broadband service, compared to just one and a half percent in urban areas
- In a 2024 study, the FCC reported that 45 million Americans lack access to the minimum standard of 100 Mbps download and 20 Mbps upload

**3.2 The BEAD Program: How Policy Shifts Reduced Coverage**
- The $42.45 billion Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment program has seen administrative changes that disqualified hundreds of thousands of locations from receiving internet access by shifting preference away from fiber infrastructure toward lower-cost options including fixed wireless and satellite systems
- Missouri received $1.7 billion allocation from BEAD, the third largest in the nation behind California and Texas

**3.3 Economic Returns from Connectivity Investment**
- Research demonstrates that a ten percent increase in broadband access generates a 1.2% increase in GDP per capita
- Rural areas achieving over eighty percent broadband adoption experience 213% higher business growth rates, forty-four percent higher GDP growth, and eighteen percent higher per capita income growth compared to areas with lower adoption

**3.4 Permitting Timelines and Implementation Failures**
- Current program timelines fail to account for the four years required for permitting processes in many jurisdictions
- Missouri's $1.7 billion broadband deployment targets more than 200,000 unserved and underserved locations
- According to the U.S. Census Bureau, there are over 687,000 Missouri households (28% of all households) with income less than $35,000 per year

### Chapter 4: Opportunity Cost and Strategic Misallocation: The Golden Dome Case

**4.1 Space-Based Missile Defense: $75-175 Billion for Speculative Security**
- The Golden Dome initiative carries initial cost estimates between seventy-five and one hundred seventy-five billion dollars, with some projections extending into the trillions over a twenty-year deployment horizon

**4.2 Technical Feasibility and the Star Wars Precedent**

**4.4 What Are We Protecting? Walls vs. Bridges**

- The Inflation Reduction Act authorizes approximately three hundred ninety billion dollars in energy security and climate investment over ten years. Golden Dome initial deployment costs would exceed two to four years of total IRA climate investment

### Chapter 5: The Electric Vehicle Decarbonization Gap

**5.1 Grid Composition and Emission Reality: 60% Fossil Fuel Dependency**

**5.2 The Suppressed Diagonal: Fossil Fuels to EVs**

**5.5 Incumbent Advantage: Utilities, Natural Gas, and EV Manufacturers**

### Chapter 6: Municipal Solid Waste: The 812-Kilogram American

**6.1 Global Waste Generation Patterns and U.S. Exceptionalism**
- In the 2025 Global Waste Index, the United States averaged 951 kilograms per capita annually (up from over 800 kg in the 2019 report)
- Americans generate more waste than any other nation in the world, officially with 4.4 pounds (2.0 kg) of municipal solid waste (MSW) per person per day, with another study estimating 7.1 pounds (3.2 kg) per capita per day

**6.2 Should We Gild the Darkness? Consumption as Inverted Achievement**

**6.3 The 162-Million-Ton Feedstock Opportunity**
- In 2018, the total generation of MSW in the U.S. reached a high of 292 million tons
- Approximately 69 million tons of MSW were recycled, 25 million tons were composted, and 35 million tons were combusted for energy recovery, which equates to 44.1% of this generated MSW being productively reused

**6.4 Environmental Justice and Waste Infrastructure Siting**
- Fifty five percent of this waste is contributed as residential garbage, while the remaining forty five percent comes from manufacturing, retailing, and commercial trade

**6.5 Waste-to-Energy Policy Failures and Recovery Potential**
- According to the Global Waste Management Outlook 2024 produced by UNEP, the generation of municipal solid waste will increase by nearly 2 billion tons and hit 3.8 billion tons by 2050
- If waste management problems are not addressed immediately, the cost of waste and its management could almost triple by 2050 to an astounding US$640.3 billion (in 2020, this was US$252 billion)
