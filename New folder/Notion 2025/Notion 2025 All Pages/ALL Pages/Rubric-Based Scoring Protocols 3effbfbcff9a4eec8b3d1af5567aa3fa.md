# Rubric-Based Scoring Protocols

Standardized criteria and scoring procedures based on major assessment frameworks.

---

## Core Assessment Dimensions

### 1. Content & Task Fulfillment

**Evaluation criteria:**

- Does the text address the intended topic comprehensively?
- Are ideas substantive and well-developed?
- Is the argument/thesis clear and well-supported?

**Theoretical frameworks:**

- **Toulmin's Model**: Check for clear Claims, Grounds (Evidence), and Warrants
- **Bloom's Taxonomy**: Assess cognitive level (Analysis/Synthesis/Evaluation > Knowledge/Comprehension)

**Scoring indicators:**

- **High**: Complex argument with multiple well-supported claims; higher-order thinking evident
- **Medium**: Adequate development with some support; mostly application-level thinking
- **Low**: Minimal development; primarily recall or description

### 2. Organization & Coherence

**Sub-dimensions:**

**Structural:**

- Logical flow and progression
- Clear intro/body/conclusion
- Effective transitions
- Unity across sections

**Entity-based:**

- Consistent entity references
- Smooth anaphora
- Natural entity transitions

**Semantic:**

- Topic consistency
- Thematic continuity
- Appropriate lexical overlap

**Discourse:**

- Appropriate connectives
- Logical relations
- Rhetorical structure

**Scoring indicators:**

- **High (0.75-1.0)**: "Well organized and unified; ideas flow logically with effective transitions"
- **Medium (0.45-0.74)**: "Adequate organization with some gaps; reader can follow but with effort"
- **Low (0.0-0.44)**: "Lacks clear organization; reader has difficulty following argument"

### 3. Development & Support

**Evaluation criteria:**

- Sufficient examples and evidence
- Adequate detail and explanation
- Logical reasoning connecting claims to support

**SOLO Taxonomy levels:**

- **Extended Abstract**: Generalizes to broader principles; transferable understanding
- **Relational**: Integrates multiple aspects into coherent whole (target for high scores)
- **Multi-structural**: Covers multiple points but unintegrated (mid-range)
- **Uni-structural**: Single aspect only (low)
- **Pre-structural**: Irrelevant or no understanding (fail)

### 4. Language Use & Style

**Vocabulary:**

- Sophistication and range
- Precision
- Domain-appropriate terminology

**Grammar & Mechanics:**

- Subject-verb agreement
- Tense consistency
- Punctuation and spelling
- Sentence variety

**Style:**

- Appropriate tone
- Clear expression
- Sentence fluency
- Minimal redundancy

## Standardized Rubric Reference

### TOEFL iBT Writing

**Scale**: 0-5 (holistic, two tasks combined → 0-30)

**Score 5 (High)**:

- "Well organized and well developed"
- "Displays unity, progression, and coherence"
- Strong language control with minor errors

**Score 3 (Medium)**:

- "Displays unity and progression but may have inconsistencies"
- "May demonstrate limited development"
- Noticeable errors that do not obscure meaning

**Score 1 (Low)**:

- "Incoherent or disorganized"
- "Inadequate organization or connection of ideas"
- Serious language errors impede communication

**Key criteria**: Development, organization, language use, grammatical fluency

### IELTS Academic Writing

**Scale**: Band 1-9 (analytic, four criteria)

**Four equally weighted criteria:**

1. **Task Response**
    - Band 9: Fully addresses all parts; well-developed position
    - Band 6: Addresses task; mostly relevant but may lack focus
    - Band 4: Minimal response; format inappropriate
2. **Coherence & Cohesion** ⭐
    - Band 9: "Uses cohesion effortlessly; sequences information logically"
    - Band 7: "Logically organizes; clear progression throughout"
    - Band 5: "Lack of overall progression; inadequate cohesive devices"
    - Band 4: "Not arranged coherently; no clear progression"
3. **Lexical Resource**
    - Band 9: Full flexibility; rare errors
    - Band 6: Adequate range; some inaccuracies
    - Band 4: Very limited range; errors obscure meaning
4. **Grammatical Range & Accuracy**
    - Band 9: Full range; error-free
    - Band 6: Mix of simple/complex; errors don't impede
    - Band 4: Very limited structures; frequent errors

**Note**: Coherence separated from grammar—grammatically perfect but poorly organized = low band possible

### GRE Analytical Writing

**Scale**: 0-6 in 0.5 increments (holistic)

**Score 6 (Outstanding)**:

- "Sustains a well-focused, well-organized analysis"
- Insightful reasoning and compelling support
- Clear, fluent expression
- Minor flaws acceptable

**Score 5 (Strong)**:

- "Is well focused and organized"
- Generally thoughtful analysis
- Clear control of language
- Minor errors acceptable

**Score 3 (Limited)**:

- "Is limited in focus and/or organization"
- Weak analysis or support
- Language impedes clarity
- Frequent errors

**Score 2 (Seriously Flawed)**:

- "Lacks organization or coherence"
- Little or no development
- Serious language problems

**Key focus**: Quality of analysis, organization, clarity, fluency

## Scoring Interpretation

### Holistic vs. Analytic

**Holistic Scoring (GRE, TOEFL)**:

- **Process**: Single overall impression-based score
- **Advantage**: Quick, efficient for large-scale assessment
- **Limitation**: Less diagnostic; doesn't pinpoint specific weaknesses
- **Use case**: High-stakes summative assessment

**Analytic Scoring (IELTS, SAT)**:

- **Process**: Separate scores for defined criteria
- **Advantage**: Diagnostic feedback; identifies strengths/weaknesses
- **Limitation**: More time-intensive
- **Use case**: Formative assessment, instructional settings

### Score Mapping

**From automated metrics to rubric levels:**

**Capacity-Weighted Jaccard → Organization score**:

- 0.75-1.0 → IELTS Band 8-9 / TOEFL 5 / GRE 5-6
    - "Well organized and unified"
    - "Effortless cohesion"
- 0.45-0.74 → IELTS Band 6-7 / TOEFL 3-4 / GRE 3-4
    - "Adequate organization with some gaps"
    - "Mostly coherent progression"
- 0.0-0.44 → IELTS Band 4-5 / TOEFL 1-2 / GRE 1-2
    - "Lacks clear organization"
    - "Reader has difficulty following"

**Calibration process**:

1. Validate on known datasets (ASAP, TOEFL11, GCDC)
2. Compute correlation with human scores
3. Adjust thresholds for maximum alignment
4. Test inter-rater reliability

### Multifaceted Profile Reporting

**Example output:**

```
Overall Quality: 7.2/10

Dimension Breakdown:
├─ Reference cohesion:     0.85 (High)
├─ Lexical cohesion:       0.60 (Moderate)
├─ Entity continuity:      0.72 (Moderate-High)
├─ Semantic coherence:     0.68 (Moderate)
└─ Discourse connectivity: 0.30 (Low) ⚠️

Action Items:
• Improve transitions and logical connectors
• Add discourse markers (however, therefore, etc.)
• Strengthen paragraph-to-paragraph links
```

## Application Procedures

### For Holistic Scoring

**Weighted combination approach**:

```
Overall = w₁(content) + w₂(organization) + w₃(language) + w₄(development)
```

**Suggested weights** (based on rubric emphasis):

- Content: 30%
- Organization/Coherence: 25-30%
- Language use: 20-25%
- Development: 20%

**Coherence penalty**: Low coherence (<0.4) should cap overall score at mid-level, regardless of other factors

### For Analytic Scoring

**Procedure**:

1. Score each dimension separately using automated + manual checks
2. Map each dimension score to rubric level
3. Provide dimension-specific feedback
4. Calculate composite if needed

**Example (IELTS-style)**:

- Task Response: 7 (automated relevance + manual judgment)
- Coherence & Cohesion: 6 (capacity-weighted Jaccard + entity grid)
- Lexical Resource: 7 (vocabulary analyzer)
- Grammatical Range: 6 (grammar checker)
- **Overall Band**: 6.5 (average)

### Hybrid Automated/Manual

**Use automated metrics as:**

1. **Pre-screening**: Flag texts with low coherence for manual review
2. **Second rater**: If human raters disagree, use automated score as tiebreaker
3. **Feature in ML model**: Combine with other features for final prediction
4. **Feedback generation**: Identify specific areas needing improvement

**Human override**: Always allow expert grader to override automated scores when justified

## Annotation Guidelines

### Coherence Annotation (Human Raters)

**Definition**: "Logical connectedness of ideas; the degree to which a reader can follow the text's argument/narrative flow"

**Scale**: 1-5 (or 1-9 for finer granularity)

**Anchor points**:

- **5 (Excellent)**: Ideas flow seamlessly; clear progression; effective transitions; reader never confused
- **4 (Good)**: Generally coherent; occasional minor gaps; mostly clear progression
- **3 (Adequate)**: Some coherence issues; reader can follow with effort; noticeable gaps
- **2 (Poor)**: Frequently incoherent; jumps between ideas; difficult to follow
- **1 (Very Poor)**: Incoherent throughout; no clear progression; reader lost

**Training protocol**:

1. Review 10-20 calibration texts with known scores
2. Practice scoring on 5 texts; compare with expert
3. Discuss disagreements
4. Achieve ≥0.7 weighted kappa before live scoring

### Inter-Rater Reliability

**Target metrics**:

- Weighted Kappa: ≥0.7 (substantial agreement)
- Exact agreement: ≥60%
- Adjacent agreement: ≥90%

**Quality assurance**:

- Double-score 20% of texts
- Third adjudicator if scores differ by ≥2 points
- Regular calibration meetings

## Validity & Fairness

### Construct Validity

**Ensure metrics measure coherence, not confounds**:

- ❌ Essay length (longer ≠ more coherent)
- ❌ Vocabulary sophistication alone
- ❌ Native language background
- ✅ Logical structure and flow
- ✅ Idea connectivity
- ✅ Reader comprehensibility

### Fairness Considerations

**Check for bias**:

- Analyze scores by demographic subgroups
- Ensure no systematic advantage/disadvantage
- Test on diverse corpora (TOEFL11 has 11 L1 backgrounds)

**Mitigation**:

- Use content-independent features when possible
- Combine multiple complementary metrics
- Human review of borderline cases
