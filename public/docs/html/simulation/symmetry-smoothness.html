Symmetry as the Organizing Principle of Smooth Motion

Smoothness = continuity + symmetry + minimum variation. Continuity (C1, C2) guarantees derivatives exist; symmetry organizes them into balanced patterns. Below, a minimum-snap lane change on the planning-level model x = [X, Y, ψ, v, a, δ]⊤, u = [j, δ]⊤ — with a knob ε that breaks the symmetry while preserving C².

Optimization — Euler–Lagrange generates symmetry
J = ∫₀T j² dt  or  J = ∫₀T δ² dt
Whenever boundary conditions and constraints are symmetric, the Euler–Lagrange equations of these functionals produce symmetric extremals. Panel C sweeps the asymmetry ε — the cost minimum sits exactly at the symmetric maneuver.
Group structure — SE(2) and the two mirrors
The kinematic bicycle is (approximately) invariant under the planar Euclidean group SE(2): translations and rotations. Reflection (x, y) ↦ (x, −y) maps a left lane change onto a right one; time reversal t ↦ T−t maps the exit onto the entry. Planning exploits symmetries of configuration space, time, and control space at once.
