# Measure-Theoretic Multi-Scale Compositional Framework

## Purpose

Rigorous measure-theoretic reformulation of the multi-scale compositional framework using σ-algebras, filtrations, and Radon-Nikodym derivatives while maintaining the **boundary condition interpretation** (Option A) of $w_{text{mwt}}$.

This formulation provides canonical mathematical foundations that connect to Bayesian non-parametrics, topological data analysis, and stochastic processes, as documented in [Report on mathematical techniques](https://www.notion.so/Report-on-mathematical-techniques-2baf832e52ca808f977fc5da7645e92f?pvs=21).

- **Automatic conservation** through countable additivity of measures

- **Scale hierarchy as filtration** aligning with hierarchical Dirichlet processes and persistent homology

## Core Structural Changes

### 1. Replace Discrete Scales with a Filtration of σ-Algebras

**Current approach:**

- Discrete scale levels $\Phi = \{\phi_0, \phi_1, \phi_2, \ldots, \phi_n\}$
- Implicit containment relationships

**Measure-theoretic rewrite:**

Define a **decreasing filtration** of σ-algebras on the document space $Omega$:

$$
\mathcal{F}_0 \supset \mathcal{F}_1 \supset \mathcal{F}_2 \supset \cdots \supset \mathcal{F}_n
$$

where:

- $\Omega$ = the entire document as a measurable space
- $\mathcal{F}_0 = \{\emptyset, \Omega\}$ = trivial σ-algebra (root scale: only the whole document)
- $\mathcal{F}_1$ = σ-algebra generated by Parts
- $\mathcal{F}_2$ = σ-algebra generated by Chapters (finer partition)
- $\mathcal{F}_k$ = σ-algebra generated by nodes at scale $k$
- $\mathcal{F}_n$ = finest partition (atomic segments with $w geq w_{text{mwt}}$)

**Key property:** Each $\mathcal{F}_k$ is generated by a **partition** of $\Omega$ into disjoint measurable sets (the nodes at that scale).

### 2. Define Word Count as a Finite Measure

**Current approach:**

- Word count field $w_{i,k}$ with additive conservation law

**Measure-theoretic rewrite:**

Define a **finite measure** $\mu_w$ on $(Omega, mathcal{F}_n)$:

$$
\mu_w: \mathcal{F}_n \to [0, W_{\text{total}}]
$$

**Properties:**

- **Countable additivity:** For disjoint sets $A_1, A_2, ldots in mathcal{F}_n$:
  
    $$
    \mu_w\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty} A_i\right) = \sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \mu_w(A_i)
    $$

- **Normalization:** $\mu_w(\Omega) = W_{\text{total}}$

- **Interpretation:** For any measurable set $A$ (a node, chapter, or segment), $\mu_w(A)$ is the word count of $A$

This **automatically** enforces the conservation law: if a parent node $P$ has disjoint children ${C_1, C_2, ldots, C_m}$, then:

$$
\mu_w(P) = \sum_{j=1}^{m} \mu_w(C_j)
$$

### 3. Define Tone Measures with Radon-Nikodym Derivatives

**Current approach:**

- Compositional state tensor $\mathbf{C}_{i,k}$ with fractional compositions $c_{i,k}^{(j)}$

**Measure-theoretic rewrite:**

For each tone type $tau_j in mathcal{T} = {tau_1, ldots, tau_m}$, define a **finite measure** $\mu_j$ on $(Omega, mathcal{F}_n)$:

$$
\mu_j(A) = \text{total word count of tone } \tau_j \text{ in set } A
$$

**Absolute continuity:** Each $\mu_j \ll \mu_w$ (absolutely continuous with respect to word count).

**Compositional ratios as Radon-Nikodym derivatives:**

$$
c^{(j)} = \frac{d\mu_j}{d\mu_w}
$$

For a measurable set $A$, the composition of tone $\tau_j$ in $A$ is:

$$
c_A^{(j)} = \frac{\mu_j(A)}{\mu_w(A)} \quad \text{(when } \mu_w(A) > 0\text{)}
$$

**Normalization constraint** becomes:

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{m} \mu_j(A) = \mu_w(A) \quad \forall A \in \mathcal{F}_n
$$

or equivalently:

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{m} \frac{d\mu_j}{d\mu_w} = 1 \quad \mu_w\text{-almost everywhere}
$$

### 4. Minimum Compositional Unit as Measure Constraint (Option A)

**Boundary condition interpretation:**

Define the **toneable domain** as:

$$
\Omega_{\text{toneable}} = \{x \in \Omega : x \text{ belongs to a segment } S \text{ with } \mu_w(S) \geq w_{\text{mwt}}\}
$$

The tone measures $\mu_j$ are **well-defined only on** $Omega_{text{toneable}}$.

**Atomic σ-algebra:** $\mathcal{F}_n$ is generated by the partition into:

- Natural segments (paragraphs, subsections) with $\mu_w(S) \geq w_{\text{mwt}}$
- Aggregated segments (small units combined to exceed threshold)

**Key:** Segments can be **variable-sized**, respecting document structure.

### 5. Conditional Expectations for Scale Hierarchy

**Current approach:**

- Implicit parent-child composition relationships
- Vertical shear $\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\text{vert}}_{i,k} = \mathbf{C}_{i,k} - \mathbf{C}_{\text{parent}(i),k-1}$

**Measure-theoretic rewrite:**

For tone $tau_j$, define the **conditional expectation** of the composition at scale $k$ given scale $k-1$:

$$
\mathbb{E}[c^{(j)} \mid \mathcal{F}_{k-1}](x) = \frac{\mu_j(A_{k-1}(x))}{\mu_w(A_{k-1}(x))}
$$

where $A_{k-1}(x)$ is the unique set in $\mathcal{F}_{k-1}$ containing point $x$.

**Vertical shear** becomes the **martingale increment:**

$$
\Delta_k c^{(j)} = c_{\mathcal{F}_k}^{(j)} - \mathbb{E}[c_{\mathcal{F}_k}^{(j)} \mid \mathcal{F}_{k-1}]
$$

**Interpretation:**

- Zero shear ⟺ composition is a **martingale** (child inherits parent composition exactly)
- Non-zero shear ⟺ **deviation from conditional expectation** (specialized child)

### 6. Disintegration for Parent-Child Relationships

**Current approach:**

- Forward mode: allocate word counts with $w_{i,k} = \alpha_{i,k} \cdot w_{\text{parent}}$

**Measure-theoretic rewrite:**

Use **disintegration of measures** (analogous to conditional probability):

Given a parent set $P \in \mathcal{F}_{k-1}$ with children ${C_1, ldots, C_m} subset mathcal{F}_k$, the word count measure **disintegrates** as:

$$
\mu_w = \int_{\mathcal{F}_{k-1}} \mu_w^{A}(·) \, d\nu(A)
$$

where $\mu_w^{A}$ is the **conditional measure** on children of $A$, and $\nu$ is the "base" measure on the parent partition.

**Practical form:** For parent $P$ and child $C subset P$:

$$
\mu_w(C) = \alpha_C \cdot \mu_w(P), \quad \sum_{C \subset P} \alpha_C = 1
$$

**Tone allocation:**

$$
\mu_j(C) = c_C^{(j)} \cdot \mu_w(C)
$$

subject to:

$$
\sum_{C \subset P} \mu_j(C) = \mu_j(P) \quad \text{(tone conservation)}
$$

## Rewritten Core Definitions

### Definition 1: Document Measure Space

The document is a **finite measure space** $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}_n, \mu_w)$ where:

- $\Omega$ = document text
- $\mathcal{F}_n$ = σ-algebra generated by toneable segments
- $\mu_w$ = word-count measure with $\mu_w(\Omega) = W_{\text{total}}$

### Definition 2: Scale Filtration

A **decreasing filtration** $\{\mathcal{F}_k\}_{k=0}^n$ with $\mathcal{F}_0 \supset \mathcal{F}_1 \supset \cdots \supset \mathcal{F}_n$ where each $\mathcal{F}_k$ is generated by a partition of $\Omega$ into scale-$k$ nodes.

### Definition 3: Tone Measure System

A collection $\{\mu_j\}_{j=1}^m$ of finite measures on $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}_n)$ satisfying:

1. **Absolute continuity:** $\mu_j \ll \mu_w$ for all $j$
2. **Completeness:** $\sum_{j=1}^m \mu_j = \mu_w$
3. **Non-negativity:** $\mu_j(A) \geq 0$ for all measurable $A$

### Definition 4: Composition Function

For a measurable set $A \in \mathcal{F}_n$ with $mu_w(A) > 0$, the **composition vector** is:

$$
\mathbf{c}_A = \begin{bmatrix} c_A^{(1)} \\ \vdots \\ c_A^{(m)} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{\mu_1(A)}{\mu_w(A)} \\ \vdots \\ \frac{\mu_m(A)}{\mu_w(A)} \end{bmatrix}
$$

This is the **Radon-Nikodym derivative** $mathbf{c}_A = frac{dboldsymbol{mu}}{dmu_w}big|_A$.

## Rewritten Key Operations

### Forward Mode (Target Planning)

**Input:** Target compositions $\mathbf{c}_A^{\text{target}}$ for all $A in mathcal{F}_k$, $k = 0, \ldots, n$

**Procedure:**

1. **Initialize root measure:**
   
    $$
    \mu_w(\Omega) = W_{\text{total}}, \quad \mu_j(\Omega) = c_{\Omega}^{(j)} \cdot W_{\text{total}}
    $$

2. **Disintegrate across scales:**
   
    For $k = 0$ to $n-1$, for each parent $P in mathcal{F}_k$:
   
   - Specify allocation fractions $\{\alpha_C : C \text{ child of } P\}$ with $\sum \alpha_C = 1$
   - Set $\mu_w(C) = \alpha_C \cdot \mu_w(P)$
   - Set $\mu_j(C) = c_C^{(j),\text{target}} \cdot \mu_w(C)$
   - **Verify:** $\sum_C \mu_j(C) = \mu_j(P)$ (if not, adjust $c_C^{(j),text{target}}$)

3. **Verify minimum threshold:**
   
    For all atomic segments $S in mathcal{F}*n$: $mu_w(S) geq w*{text{mwt}}$

### Reverse Mode (Empirical Analysis)

**Input:** Existing document text

**Procedure:**

1. **Segment document:**
   
    Partition $\Omega$ into natural segments $\{S_1, \ldots, S_N\}$ where each $\mu_w(S_i) \geq w_{\text{mwt}}$ or is aggregated

2. **Measure segments:**
   
   - Compute $\mu_w(S_i)$ for each segment
   - Classify dominant tone: $\tau(S_i) \in \mathcal{T}$
   - Set $\mu_{\tau(S_i)}(S_i) = \mu_w(S_i)$ and $\mu_j(S_i) = 0$ for $j \neq \tau(S_i)$ (single-tone constraint)

3. **Extend measures to coarser scales:**
   
    For each $A \in \mathcal{F}_k$ with $k < n$:
   
    $$
    \mu_w(A) = \sum_{S_i \subset A} \mu_w(S_i), \quad \mu_j(A) = \sum_{S_i \subset A} \mu_j(S_i)
    $$

4. **Compute compositions:**
   
    $$
    \mathbf{c}_A = \frac{1}{\mu_w(A)} \begin{bmatrix} \mu_1(A) \\ \vdots \\ \mu_m(A) \end{bmatrix}
    $$

### Adaptive Mode (Rebalancing)

**Measure perturbation:**

$$
\mu_w^{\text{new}}(A) = \mu_w^{\text{old}}(A) + \Delta\mu_w(A)
$$

subject to:

- Conservation at parent level: $\sum_{C \subset P} \Delta\mu_w(C) = 0$
- Non-negativity: $\mu_w^{\text{new}}(A) \geq w_{\text{mwt}}$ for atomic segments

**Tone reallocation:**

$$
\mu_j^{\text{new}}(A) = c_A^{(j),\text{new}} \cdot \mu_w^{\text{new}}(A)
$$

## Key Advantages

### 1. Rigorous Foundation

σ-algebras and measures provide a standard mathematical framework with well-established theory from real analysis and probability theory.

### 2. Automatic Conservation

Countable additivity of measures eliminates manual verification of word-count conservation—it's a **theorem**, not a protocol step.

### 3. Natural Composition Definition

Radon-Nikodym derivatives are the canonical way to define "relative proportion" in measure theory.

### 4. Scale Hierarchy as Filtration

Aligns with:

- **Bayesian non-parametrics** (hierarchical Dirichlet processes)
- **Topological data analysis** (persistent homology filtrations)
- Stochastic processes (martingales, conditional expectations)

As documented in [Report on mathematical techniques](https://www.notion.so/Report-on-mathematical-techniques-2baf832e52ca808f977fc5da7645e92f?pvs=21).

### 5. Conditional Expectations

Provides a principled definition of "expected child composition given parent"—the shear tensor measures deviation from this expectation.

### 6. Extensibility

Natural paths to:

- **Probability measures** (if tones have uncertainty)
- **Stochastic composition models** (time-varying measures)
- **Machine learning frameworks** (hierarchical models, deep learning)

### 7. Research Literature Connection

Directly relates to random measures, filtrations, and disintegration concepts in:

- Jordan et al. on completely random measures and hierarchical Dirichlet processes
- Blumberg et al. on persistent homology for metric measure spaces
- Topological filtration learning for graph neural networks
- Variational segmentation in measure spaces

## Extensions Enabled by Measure Theory

### 1. Stochastic Composition Models

Replace deterministic measures with **random measures**:

$$
\mu_j \sim \text{Gamma Process}(\alpha, H)
$$

where $H$ is a base measure and $\alpha$ controls concentration.

### 2. Bayesian Hierarchical Models

Use **hierarchical Dirichlet processes** to learn composition distributions:

$$
G_0 \sim \text{DP}(\alpha_0, H), \quad G_k \sim \text{DP}(\alpha_k, G_0)
$$

where $G_0$ is the global tone distribution and $G_k$ are scale-specific distributions.

### 3. Persistent Homology for Composition

Treat the filtration $\{\mathcal{F}_k\}$ as a topological filtration and compute **persistence barcodes** for compositional features:

- Birth: scale at which a compositional pattern emerges
- Death: scale at which the pattern disappears
- Persistence: death − birth (robustness of the pattern)

### 4. Uncertainty Quantification

Define probability measures on composition space:

$$
\mathbb{P}(c^{(j)} \in [a, b]) = \int_a^b p(c^{(j)}) \, dc^{(j)}
$$

Use Wasserstein or Prohorov metrics to compare composition distributions across scales.

### 5. Time Evolution

Extend to measure-valued processes:

$$
\mu_j(A, t) : \mathcal{F}_n \times [0, T] \to \mathbb{R}_+
$$

Track how tone measures evolve across document versions.
