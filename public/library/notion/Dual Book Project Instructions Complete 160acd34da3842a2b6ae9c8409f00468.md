## 📖 THE TWO BOOKS: PARALLEL MISSIONS

### **BOOK 1: PROSECUTING INEQUITY (Climate Investment)**

**Full Title:** *Prosecuting Inequity: Climate Investment, Social Decay, and the Architecture of Equitable Decarbonization*

**Core Thesis:** Climate investment programs have systematically failed equity objectives by concentrating benefits among wealthy households and incumbent energy producers. The book integrates policy architecture with biomass-to-hydrogen techno-economics to demonstrate how scarce public decarbonization funds should be allocated.

**Target Audience:**

- University press publication
- Policy institute reports
- Graduate-level courses in energy policy, environmental justice, institutional economics
- General educated audience interested in climate policy and inequality

**Structure:** 28 chapters across 5 parts + appendices (400-500 pages)

**Key Themes:**

- Dollar decline and fiscal constraints
- Educational infrastructure collapse
- Digital divide and rural exclusion
- EV decarbonization gap
- Municipal solid waste as energy feedstock
- Religious institutional accountability
- Clean energy credit concentration
- Biomass gasification techno-economics
- Reusable capital and parliament-based allocation

### **BOOK 2: DISHONEST AI IS DANGEROUS AI (AI Ethics)**

**Full Title:** *Dishonest AI is Dangerous AI: Accountability, Equity, and Technical Transparency in Artificial Intelligence*

**Core Thesis:** Algorithmic dishonesty represents a systemic threat to democratic governance, economic equity, and technical safety. The book prosecutes AI deployment failures through the same equity, capacity, and resource allocation frameworks used for climate investment.

**Target Audience:**

- University press publication
- Policy institute reports
- Graduate-level courses in CS ethics, AI governance, technology policy

**Structure:** 28 chapters across 5 parts + appendices (400-500 pages)

## 📋 CHAPTER TAGGING SYSTEM (BOTH BOOKS)

Each chapter in both books uses consistent reader-priority tags:

### **Tag Definitions:**

| Tag | Meaning | Guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CRITICAL** | Essential reading for this reader type | Must include full depth and detail |
| **IMPORTANT** | Strongly recommended, adds significant value | Include substantial content, can be slightly condensed |
| **OPTIONAL** | Helpful context but not essential | Brief treatment, focus on takeaways |
| **SKIP** | Not relevant for this reader type | Minimal/no coverage for this audience |

### **Multi-Tag Interpretation:**

**When a chapter has multiple CRITICAL tags:**

- Requires MORE depth, length, and development
- Must serve multiple audiences simultaneously
- Use clear sectioning to separate technical vs. accessible content
- Consider “pathway boxes” or “reader notes”

**When a chapter has mostly SKIP tags:**

- Candidate for condensing if page count is tight
- Focus ONLY on the 1-2 reader types marked CRITICAL/IMPORTANT
- Keep focused and efficient

**When tags conflict (CRITICAL for one type, SKIP for another):**

- Use modular structure with clearly marked sections
- Technical readers get full depth in dedicated sections
- General readers get accessible overview with skip guidance

## 🎨 CONTENT CALIBRATION GUIDELINES

### **For CLIMATE BOOK Chapters:**

**When writing for Policy Makers:**

- Focus on: Implications, cost-benefit, implementation feasibility
- Avoid: Deep technical detail, equations, process diagrams
- Include: Dollar figures, timeline estimates, accountability metrics

**When writing for Technical Specialists:**

- Focus on: TEA methodology, scaling exponents, LCA boundaries
- Include: Equations, sensitivity analyses, comparative benchmarks
- Reference: NREL, NETL, Argonne GREET models

**When writing for Rural Stakeholders:**

- Focus on: Feedstock pricing, logistics, local employment
- Avoid: Academic framing, policy jargon, abstract theory
- Include: Specific farm/forestry examples, revenue projections

**When writing for Religious Leaders:**

- Focus on: Community benefit, accountability metrics, institutional capacity
- Frame as: Stewardship, prevention, measurable outcomes
- Include: Local impact examples, performance dashboards

### **For AI ETHICS BOOK Chapters:**

**When writing for Policy Makers/Regulators:**

- Focus on: Enforcement mechanisms, market structure, antitrust tools
- Avoid: Deep ML technical details, model architectures
- Include: Precedent cases, regulatory frameworks, accountability levers

**When writing for AI/ML Engineers:**

- Focus on: Computational efficiency, auditing protocols, carbon footprint
- Include: Architecture details, optimization strategies, benchmarks
- Reference: MLPerf, OpenAI technical reports, efficiency research

**When writing for Workers/Communities:**

- Focus on: Job impacts, displacement patterns, organizing strategies
- Avoid: Technical jargon, academic framing
- Include: Real worker stories, organizing toolkits, demand templates

**When writing for Civil Society/Labor:**

- Focus on: Leverage points, coalition strategies, accountability demands
- Frame as: Power analysis, campaign targets, measurable wins
- Include: Organizing case studies, template agreements

## 📊 PAGE COUNT MANAGEMENT (BOTH BOOKS)

Both books target **400-500 pages**. Page allocation must be strategic:

### **Strategic Allocation Principles:**

**High-Priority Chapters** (multiple CRITICAL tags):

- Climate Ch 1, 2, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 20, 21, 24, 28
- AI Ethics Ch 1, 2, 5, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12, 20, 21, 22, 23, 28
- **Allocation:** 15-25 pages each

**Technical Depth Chapters** (CRITICAL for specialists, SKIP for general):

- Climate Part III (Ch 13-19)
- AI Ethics Part III (Ch 13-19)
- **Allocation:** 12-20 pages each (includes equations, tables, figures)

**Low-Priority Chapters** (mostly OPTIONAL/SKIP):

- Candidates for condensing if needed
- **Allocation:** 8-12 pages

### **Page Count Monitoring:**

**Tools:**

- Track chapter word counts in Asana task notes
- Use standard academic formatting: ~350 words/page
- Target ranges: 140,000-175,000 words total

**Red Flags:**

- ⚠️ Book exceeding 185,000 words → Identify chapters to condense
- ⚠️ CRITICAL chapters under 4,000 words → May need expansion
- ⚠️ SKIP-heavy chapters over 7,000 words → Likely over-developed
