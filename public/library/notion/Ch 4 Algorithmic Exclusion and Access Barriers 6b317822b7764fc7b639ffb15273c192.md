**📋 CONTENT SUMMARY:**
This chapter examines how access barriers to AI tools, infrastructure, and benefits create systematic exclusion patterns paralleling the digital divide in the climate book. It documents how geographic, economic, linguistic, and technical barriers concentrate AI benefits among already-advantaged populations.

**📊 KEY DATA POINTS & MACRO FACTS:**
**Geographic Access Disparities:**
- **~85%** of global AI compute located in US, EU, China
- **<2%** of AI research papers from Africa
- **~30%** of rural US lacks broadband sufficient for cloud AI services
- Data center concentration: **~60%** in 20 metro areas globally
**Language Barriers:**
- **~60%** of AI training data in English (only ~20% of global speakers)
- **<1%** of AI datasets for languages spoken by **>50%** of global population
- Translation accuracy: **90%+** for English-Spanish, **<60%** for low-resource languages
- Voice assistants: functional in **<50** of **~7,000** human languages
**Economic Access:**
- ChatGPT Plus ($20/month) = **>week's income** for **~40%** of global population
- Enterprise AI tools: **$100-$10,000/month** per user
- API costs: **$0.002-$0.12 per 1K tokens** (prohibitive at scale for small organizations)
- **90%+** of AI startups funded in US/EU/China, **<5%** in Global South
**Infrastructure Requirements:**
- Minimum device for local AI: **$500-$1,000** smartphone/laptop (beyond reach for billions)
- Bandwidth for cloud AI: **10+ Mbps** stable (unavailable to **~40%** globally)
- Storage for AI apps: **5-50 GB** (limited on budget devices)
- Electricity reliability: intermittent power prevents **~1 billion** from AI access
**Climate Book Parallels:**
- Rural broadband gap (22% vs 1.5%) ≈ **AI tool availability** (urban 85% vs rural 40%)
- $42B BEAD reduced coverage ≈ **AI subsidies** concentrating in wealthy markets
- Permitting delays (4 years) ≈ **Deployment timelines** excluding urgent needs
- 10% GDP increase from broadband ≈ **15-25%** productivity gains from AI (where accessible)

**💡 KEY INSIGHTS BY READER TYPE:**
**For Regulators:**
- Access disparities create two-tier society (AI haves vs have-nots)
- Market alone won't solve - profitable to serve wealthy, ignore poor
- Universal service obligations for AI infrastructure (like telecom)
- Public AI infrastructure as accessibility intervention
- **Action:** Mandate accessibility standards; fund public AI access points; regulate pricing discrimination; require low-resource language support
**For Academics:**
- Research opportunities: measuring AI access gaps, documenting exclusion harms, designing inclusive systems
- Theoretical frameworks: digital divide literature, distributive justice, capability approach
- Empirical questions: How do access barriers compound? What's the welfare cost of AI exclusion?
- **Action:** Publish accessibility research; develop inclusive AI methods; partner with underserved communities; teach accessibility in CS courses
**For Public:**
- If AI tools feel out of reach, that's by design not accident
- Exclusion is political choice embedded in product decisions
- Demand for accessible AI is legitimate claim on public resources
- **Action:** Support universal AI access initiatives; use free/open alternatives; demand library AI access; organize for digital equity
**For Workers:**
- Job displacement from AI while lacking access to AI tools creates asymmetric vulnerability
- Platform workers surveilled by AI they cannot access or contest
- Skill gaps widen when training requires unaffordable tools
- **Action:** Demand employer-provided AI tools; negotiate for training access; organize for workplace AI transparency
**For Engineers:**
- Design choices create or remove access barriers
- Lightweight models enable broader access than frontier only
- Offline capability and low-bandwidth modes as equity features
- **Action:** Optimize for accessibility not just capability; support open-source alternatives; design for low-resource environments
**For Students:**
- Educational access to AI tools shapes career opportunities
- University compute resources privilege some students over others
- Free tiers and student discounts still exclude many
- **Action:** Advocate for campus AI resources; share accounts cooperatively; demand open educational AI tools
**For Organizers:**
- Access barriers create shared grievance across communities
- Coalition potential: rural broadband + AI access + platform workers
- Public libraries as organizing sites for AI access demands
- **Action:** Build digital equity coalitions; demand public AI infrastructure; organize access points in underserved areas
