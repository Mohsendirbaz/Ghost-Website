**📋 CONTENT SUMMARY:**
This chapter examines how deficits in data literacy, computational thinking, and technical expertise create power asymmetries that enable AI monopolies to operate without meaningful public oversight. It parallels the educational infrastructure collapse in the climate book, showing how capacity gaps compound monopoly concentration.

**📊 KEY DATA POINTS & MACRO FACTS:**
**Educational Access:**
- Only **~51%** of US high schools offer computer science courses (2024)
- **~35%** of rural schools offer CS vs **~55%** of urban schools
- Teacher shortage: **~50,000** unfilled CS teaching positions nationally
- CS degree gender gap: **~19%** women in CS undergrad programs
**Public Sector Capacity:**
- Federal AI/ML specialist positions: **~15,000** vs private sector **~200,000+**
- Public sector AI salary: **$80,000-$150,000** vs private **$150,000-$500,000**
- Regulatory agency AI expertise: **<100** full-time AI specialists across FTC, FCC, NIST, FDA
- Academic researcher salaries: **40-60%** of industry equivalents
**Data Literacy Deficits:**
- **~60%** of US adults cannot explain what an algorithm is
- **~45%** cannot distinguish AI-generated from human content
- **~70%** don't understand how their data is used for AI training
- **~35%** believe AI systems are objective/unbiased by default
**Brain Drain:**
- **>60%** of top AI PhD graduates go directly to industry (vs ~20% in 1990s)
- **~40%** of academic AI researchers have consulted for big tech
- University AI lab budgets: **$1-5M** vs industry lab **$50-500M**
- Faculty retention: **~30%** of top AI professors left academia for industry (2015-2023)
**Workforce Gaps:**
- Projected AI job openings: **~12 million** globally by 2025
- Current workforce with AI skills: **~3-4 million** globally
- Reskilling/upskilling needed: **~200 million** workers globally
- Training program capacity: **~10% of need**
**Climate Book Parallels:**
- 411,549 teaching positions (climate) ≈ **50,000 CS teaching gaps** (AI)
- $2,000/year social savings per graduate (climate) ≈ **$5,000/year** productivity gain per data-literate worker (AI)
- Educational ROI 25-30 year NPV (climate) ≈ **20-25 year NPV** for tech literacy (AI)

**💡 KEY INSIGHTS BY READER TYPE:**
**For Regulators:**
- Can't regulate what you don't understand - capacity building is prerequisite for oversight
- Information asymmetry enables regulatory capture by industry
- Public sector salary competitiveness essential for recruiting expertise
- Interagency coordination needed to pool scarce technical talent
- **Action:** Increase technical staff budgets; create public interest tech fellowships; mandate industry transparency to reduce expertise requirements
**For Academics:**
- Research on educational interventions for data literacy
- Documenting expertise concentration and its governance impacts
- Developing accessible explanations of AI concepts for public education
- Studying effective regulatory capacity building models
- **Action:** Offer public-facing AI literacy courses; advise government agencies; resist industry consulting conflicts; mentor public interest students
**For Students:**
- Career choice between public interest and private sector profit
- Understanding how expertise scarcity creates market power
- Educational privilege and responsibility to serve public interest
- Long-term career satisfaction vs short-term salary maximization
- **Action:** Consider public sector internships; develop teaching/communication skills; engage in policy discussions; choose mentors modeling public service
**For Public:**
- You deserve to understand systems governing your life
- Data literacy is civic literacy in 21st century
- Demand accessible explanations from tech companies and regulators
- Support educational investments in tech literacy
- **Action:** Seek out AI literacy resources; demand transparency; support education funding; participate in public comment processes
**For Workers:**
- Expertise concentration weakens worker power in AI deployment decisions
- Union capacity building needed for algorithmic accountability
- Workforce training programs chronically underfunded
- Platform workers especially vulnerable to literacy gaps
- **Action:** Demand employer-funded AI literacy training; build union technical expertise; organize for worker participation in AI governance
**For Engineers:**
- Responsibility to communicate clearly with non-technical stakeholders
- Public sector service as high-impact career path
- Teaching and mentoring as democratizing expertise
- Open-source contribution as capacity building
- **Action:** Mentor diverse students; simplify technical communications; consider government/nonprofit service; contribute to public education resources
**For Organizers:**
- Literacy gaps limit community participation in AI governance
- Building local technical capacity enables grassroots organizing
- Coalition with educators and librarians for public education
- Framing AI literacy as civil rights issue
- **Action:** Organize community AI literacy workshops; partner with libraries and schools; demand corporate-funded public education; build movement technical capacity
