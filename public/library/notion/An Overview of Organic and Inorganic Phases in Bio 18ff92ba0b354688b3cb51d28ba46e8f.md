## 1. Organic Matter

## Key Methodological Notes

### Contaminant Sources

- **a**: Contaminant in municipal solid waste
- **b**: Low-temperature ash produced in oxygen plasma (100–250°C)
- **c**: Contaminant from soil
- **d**: Additive for paper production
- **e**: Contaminant in sewage sludge as detergent, flocculant or precipitation agent
- **f**: Contaminant from fertilizers
- **g**: From animal bones
- **h**: Contaminant from sea transport

### Important Considerations

1. Leaching methods have limitations and can only provide preliminary information about element associations in phases
2. Direct methods commonly applied for coal and coal products should be used additionally for better characterization
3. Biomass contains phases of toxic elements in more mobile and hazardous forms compared to coal
4. Washing to remove water-soluble phases may reduce technological and environmental problems but creates new concerns about mobile phases
5. Some countries have regulations specifying limiting values for elements (Ca, Cd, Cl, Co, Cr, Cu, K, N, Ni, Pb, S, V, Zn) in biomass fuels

### Materials and Methods

The study includes:

- 93 varieties of biomass (197 samples)
- Data from 25 references (23 peer-reviewed articles, 1 monograph, 1 conference proceeding)
- Complete proximate, ultimate, ash, structural and extractive data for 28 biomass varieties
- Pearson's correlation test for 24 characteristics across 28 varieties of natural biomass
