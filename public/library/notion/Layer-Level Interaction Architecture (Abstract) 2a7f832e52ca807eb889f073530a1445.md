# Layer-Level Interaction Architecture (Abstract)

# Layer Interaction Architecture

This page documents **layer-level interactions** independent of specific measures or methods. Each relationship describes **functional capacities** exchanged between layers, not implementation details.

---

## Layer Definitions (Summary)

- **L1 - Lexical-Syntactic:** Surface form, vocabulary, sentence structure
- **L2 - Structural-Organizational:** Document hierarchy, section logic, referencing
- **L3 - Semantic-Entity:** Meaning stability, concept tracking, entity continuity
- **L4 - Discourse-Pragmatic:** Textual connection, flow markers, coherence bridges
- **L5 - Argumentative-Rhetorical:** Logical structure, persuasive force, claim support
- **L6 - Meta-Textual:** Global integration, genre recognition, holistic quality

---

## Pairwise Layer Interactions (15 Total)

### L1 ↔ L2: Form Constrains Structure

**Interaction:** Surface-level choices constrain and enable structural organization.

**L1 → L2 Capacities:**

- Sentence complexity determines paragraph cohesion feasibility
- Voice distribution (active/passive) affects structural prominence patterns
- Lexical variety enables structural differentiation

**L2 → L1 Capacities:**

- Hierarchical depth constrains syntactic complexity requirements
- Section boundaries require form-level signaling
- Structural parallelism demands lexical parallelism

**General Principle:** Form must be tractable enough for structure to organize; structure must be simple enough for form to express.

---

### L1 ↔ L3: Form Encodes Meaning

**Interaction:** Surface choices encode and retrieve semantic content.

**L1 → L3 Capacities:**

- Vocabulary precision determines semantic field boundaries
- Term consistency maintains concept stability
- Pronoun systems enable entity tracking

**L3 → L1 Capacities:**

- Semantic distinctions require lexical differentiation
- Entity salience demands surface prominence (subject position, frequency)
- Meaning stability constrains acceptable paraphrase

**General Principle:** Meaning differences must be encoded in form; form must be interpreted consistently.

---

### L1 ↔ L4: Form Supports Flow

**Interaction:** Surface features enable or disrupt textual connection.

**L1 → L4 Capacities:**

- Sentence rhythm affects transition smoothness
- Repetition patterns support or undermine coherence
- Readability constrains transition complexity

**L4 → L4 Capacities:**

- Discourse requirements demand explicit markers at form level
- Coherence needs constrain sentence variety tolerance
- Flow patterns require predictable form cues

**General Principle:** Flow emerges from form patterns; form must be regular enough to support flow.

### L1 ↔ L5: Form Carries Force

**L1 → L5 Capacities:**

**L5 → L1 Capacities:**

**General Principle:** Arguments are expressed through form; form choices shape persuasive impact.

## Integration Principles (Abstract)

### Principle 1: Constraint Propagation

**Direction:** Lower layers → Upper layers

**Mechanism:** Deficiencies at lower layers propagate upward

**Example:** Form errors → Structure ambiguity → Meaning confusion → Discourse breakdown → Argument failure

### Principle 2: Requirement Flow

**Direction:** Upper layers → Lower layers

**Mechanism:** Goals at upper layers impose constraints downward

**Example:** Persuasion goal → Argument needs → Discourse patterns → Semantic organization → Structural choices → Lexical selection

### Principle 3: Horizontal Coherence

**Direction:** Adjacent layers ↔ Adjacent layers

**Mechanism:** Neighboring layers must align

**Example:** Structure (L2) must support semantics (L3); semantics must enable discourse (L4); discourse must scaffold argument (L5)

### Principle 4: Diagonal Mediation

**Direction:** Non-adjacent layers through mediators

**Mechanism:** Distant layers interact via intermediate layers

**Example:** L1 affects L5 through L3 (vocabulary → entities → claims); L2 affects L6 through L4 (structure → discourse → impression)

## Optimization Strategy

### Sequential (Bottom-Up):

**Order:** L1 → L3 → L2 → L4 → L5 → L6

**Rationale:** Fix foundations before building upward

**Advantage:** Minimizes upward propagation

**Disadvantage:** May not address urgent top-level goals

### Sequential (Top-Down):

**Order:** L6 → L5 → L4 → L3 → L2 → L1

**Rationale:** Define goals before implementing details

**Advantage:** Ensures alignment with objectives

**Disadvantage:** May waste effort if foundations are broken

### Hybrid (Critical Path):

**Order:** L4 first (highest impact), then L3 (enables L4), then L5 (dependent on L4), then L1→L2→L6

**Rationale:** Address highest-coupling layers first

**Advantage:** Maximum quality gain per effort

**Disadvantage:** Requires empirical coupling data
